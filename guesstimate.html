About Guesstimate

Q1What is a guesstimate?

A guesstimate is an educated guess where you estimate a value based on logical reasoning, basic data, and assumptions. It's a combination of guessing and estimating, often used when precise data is unavailable. The goal is to approach a reasonable answer using approximations and assumptions grounded in reality.

Q2How do you solve a guesstimate question?

To solve a guesstimate question, I follow these steps:

  1. Clarify the problem: Ensure I fully understand the question and what I’m estimating.
  2. Break down the problem: Divide the question into smaller, more manageable parts.
  3. Make reasonable assumptions: Use logical assumptions for each part, ideally based on real-world knowledge.
  4. Use a structured approach: Apply math or basic data to reach a final estimate.
  5. Check the logic: Ensure the numbers make sense and that no part is overly inflated or underestimated.
Q3What function do guesstimate questions serve in Data Science?

In Data Science, guesstimate questions help assess problem-solving skills, logical thinking, and the ability to work with incomplete information—skills crucial for handling real-world data. Guesstimates are also a way to test how candidates break down complex problems into manageable parts, make reasonable assumptions, and provide justifications for their approach.

Q4What are the two methods of approaching guesstimate questions?

The two primary methods are:

  1. Top-down approach: Starting with the broadest possible estimate, and gradually narrowing it down to the finer details.
  2. Bottom-up approach: Starting with the most specific and detailed components, and then aggregating them to reach a final estimate.
Q5Explain the three different guesstimates based on how you approach a solution.
  1. Product-based guesstimate: This involves estimating based on a known product or service. For instance, estimating the number of iPhones sold by considering Apple’s sales data.
  2. Population-based guesstimate: This involves dividing the population into different segments and applying an assumption for each group. For example, estimating the number of iPhone users by segmenting the population based on age, income, and tech adoption.
  3. Resource-based guesstimate: This focuses on resources or infrastructure, such as estimating how many coffee shops are in a city by breaking down the city’s population and how often people consume coffee.
